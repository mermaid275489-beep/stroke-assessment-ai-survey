\subsection{Search and screening of AI motion-analysis evidence}
\label{sec:ai_search_methods}

To identify evidence for component-level motion quantification, we retained the legacy gait candidate pool and conducted three prespecified supplementary searches for upper-limb dexterity, trunk and balance-related movement, and strength or power. PubMed/MEDLINE was searched through NCBI E-utilities without a starting-year restriction. The search combined stroke terms, technology and quantitative-measurement terms, and stream-specific movement terms. The full search strings, retrieval dates, database counts and query URLs are archived in the supplementary search log, following PRISMA-S reporting principles and PRESS search-strategy review guidance \cite{Rethlefsen2021PRISMAS,McGowan2016PRESS}.

Records were deduplicated using DOI, PMID and normalized title--year--first-author keys. Titles and abstracts were screened using the prespecified adult-stroke, movement-task and sensor criteria, followed by full-text eligibility assessment. Tier A required an original peer-reviewed study with a separable adult stroke sample, a quantitative movement output, a computational procedure, and technical or clinical validation. Stroke measurement studies and healthy-participant technical-validation studies were retained as Tier B supporting evidence and were not counted as core stroke-AI studies. A study could map to multiple movement components but was counted once in the study-level totals.

The four new PubMed streams yielded 14,320 records; including the 474 legacy gait records, the combined source pool contained 14,794 records and 10,084 unique records after deduplication. Machine-assisted title/abstract screening identified 245 Tier A candidates, 812 Tier B candidates, 4,245 uncertain records and 4,782 title/abstract exclusions. These values are screening-pool counts rather than final inclusion counts; full-text review and adjudication must be completed before they are used in Figure~2 or reported as included studies. OpenAlex was attempted as a public cross-disciplinary source but returned HTTP 429 during the run, and subscription exports from Embase, IEEE Xplore, Scopus/Web of Science and CINAHL have not yet been merged.

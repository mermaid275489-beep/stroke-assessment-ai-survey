Stroke rehabilitation · AI motion analysis

From clinical scores to measurable movement

Clinical rating scales judge movement by eye. AI can measure it frame by frame. This project builds the bridge between the two: from motion-capture data, through a survey that decomposes 57 assessment tools into computable features, toward an app that turns ordinary video into an interpretable movement report.

THE PROBLEM WITH SCALES

Subjective, expert-dependent, hard to scale

Scores such as the Fugl-Meyer or Berg Balance Scale depend on trained raters, show ceiling effects, and are difficult to repeat often or remotely.

THE PROBLEM WITH END-TO-END AI

Predicting the score inherits its flaws

Most AI systems learn to predict the clinical score directly. They inherit its subjectivity and reveal little about which movement features drive the result.

Stage 01 · Data

Seeing the deviation in post-stroke gait

A pilot clinical gait study used optical motion capture to characterise how walking changes after stroke. Joint angles were compared with healthy controls across the whole gait cycle using statistical parametric mapping (SPM1D), which tests every percentage point of the cycle rather than a single summary value.

Stage 02 · Survey

What can a camera actually score?

Rather than treating clinical scores as prediction targets, the survey asks how each scale's scoring criteria decompose into movement features that AI can quantify objectively. We built a library of guideline-recommended and research-standard tools and coded every scoring entry.

Stage 03 · App (concept)

An AI report clinicians can read

The goal is not another black-box score. Inspired by how ECG machines print an interpretable report, the app will track movement from ordinary video, compute a task-specific feature profile, and explain deviations in clinical language, flagging what the video cannot show.

01 · AUTOMATED GAIT REPORT

From curves to a diagnosis-aware report

  • Interpreting gait biomechanics is complex and usually rule-based
  • Use language models to describe gait phenotypes such as knee hyperextension, insufficient ankle motion or a "frozen" hip
  • Benchmark general-purpose models (e.g. ChatGPT, Gemini) on describing kinematic curves
02 · MARKERLESS UPPER LIMB

Objective upper-limb tracking

  • Replace therapist-dependent tests (e.g. ARAT, WMFT) with consistent, scalable measurement
  • Consensus tasks: drinking, planar reaching and pinching
  • Multi-camera markerless systems and single-camera pose estimation (e.g. MediaPipe)
03 · SYNTHETIC DATA

More data, less identifiable

  • Address data scarcity in stroke and neurorehabilitation
  • Protect privacy: video records identifiable features
  • Generate labelled data for model testing and robotics simulation
Roadmap

Where the project stands

2020 – 2023

Clinical motion data collected

Optical motion capture and force-plate recordings of patients with stroke (upper and lower limb), knee osteoarthritis and amputation.

2025

Pilot gait analysis

Curve-level SPM1D comparison of post-stroke and healthy gait identified reduced hip and knee flexion on the affected side and delayed swing on the unaffected side.

2026

Survey: scales → computable features

Guideline and literature search (to May 2026), a 57-tool library and 196 coded entries linking clinical criteria to video availability, computability and feature families.

NEXT

Prototype app

Video → pose tracking → task-specific feature profile → interpretable report, starting with gait and upper-limb consensus tasks.

THEN

Validation and clinical utility

Analytical, task-level and clinical-construct validation across severity, assistance level, devices and settings, then evaluation of whether the report improves clinical decisions.
